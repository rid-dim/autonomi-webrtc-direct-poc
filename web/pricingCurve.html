Autonomi storage pricing curve

What a node charges to store a chunk, as a function of how much it already holds. Quadratic in the node's occupancy, with a non-zero floor.

The formula is defined once, in ant-protocol, and re-exported by ant-node rather than reimplemented. That matters because both sides compute it: the node when pricing a quote, the client when verifying the forced price before paying. A single definition means the two can never drift apart.

price_per_chunk_ANT(n) = BASELINE + K × (n / D)²

At 4 MiB chunks there are 256 chunks to a GiB, and both constants happen to be exact 256ths — so the whole thing collapses to something you can hold in your head:

ANT_per_GiB(n) = 1 + 9 × (n / 6000)²

One ANT per GiB for an empty node, ten at D. And because the term is squared, doubling what a node holds roughly quadruples what it charges. That is the property worth arguing about — not the level, but the exponent.

Why it looks like this

The two anchor points are deliberately round. An empty node charges exactly 1 ANT per GiB; at the lower stable boundary D it charges exactly 10 ANT per GiB — $0.10 and $1.00 respectively at the assumed token price. With 4 MiB chunks, D = 6000 records is roughly 23 GiB held per node, and past that the quadratic term climbs steeply.

The source comments explain what this replaced: an earlier formula produced “~$25/GB at the lower stable boundary and ~$0/GB when nodes were empty — both unreasonable.” The non-zero baseline is what stops a fresh node from offering free storage, which would otherwise be an obvious spam target.

What the exponent does in practice

A worked example, because the shape is easier to argue about with numbers attached. Take a 2.4 GiB upload and watch its cost as the nodes storing it fill up:

Node holds Records n ANT for 2.4 GiB vs. 13 GiB

So a node going from 13 to 24 GiB — not quite a doubling — takes the price for the same upload from 9 to 25 ANT, close to 3×. That is the quadratic doing exactly what a quadratic does, and it is a fair thing to raise: the objection is about the rate of change, not about whether $1/GiB is cheap for permanent storage. Both can be true at once.

Worth checking the extrapolation too, though. At a 40 GiB node the formula gives about 65 ANT for that 2.4 GiB, not hundreds — the price only reaches 200 ANT once a node is holding roughly 71 GiB. The direction of the concern holds; the specific figure overshoots by around 3×.

The inverse, and why it is written defensively

derive_records_stored_from_price() runs the formula backwards: given a quoted price, how full does the quoting node claim to be? It saturates at u64::MAX rather than overflowing, and the comment says why — it is called on untrusted, deserialized quote prices before signature verification, so a panic there would be a pre-authentication crash vector. An oversized price is left for the range check to reject instead.
